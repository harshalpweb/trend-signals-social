# Psychology of Shareable, Save-Worthy Content

Research pass: 2026-08-17. Applied specifically to data/trend-reveal content for a "receipts, not hype" brand.

## 1. STEPPS (Jonah Berger), applied to data reveals

- **Social Currency + Practical Value are the primary levers** — people share stats that make them look informed. Frame as "here's what's actually moving," evidence-first, not aspirational. [fastcompany.com/3005679]
- **Triggers + Stories**: pair raw data with a one-line context, not a full narrative arc — "3 competing platforms now require X; here's the data." [unruly.co]
- **Public**: bold, readable, well-sourced visuals signal authority by themselves — visible source credit reads as rigor, not clutter.

## 2. Curiosity-gap hooks

- First slide should never reveal the insight — open a loop the swipe resolves. Posts using curiosity gaps see meaningfully higher engagement than posts that front-load the answer. [imagine.art/blogs/best-carousel-hooks]
- Resolve the gap honestly by slide 2-3. Fake urgency ("this just happened!") is out of bounds for this brand — use truthful framing instead ("we tracked this for 6 months").
- Because Instagram can re-serve carousels with slide 2 as the new entry point, slide 2 needs to be able to hook on its own, not just pay off slide 1.

## 3. Saves vs. likes

- Saves signal reference value ("I'll come back to this / forward it"); likes signal momentary approval. Data content should over-index on saves — that's correct for this brand, not underperformance.
- Saves are reported to outweigh likes in distribution algorithms — a direct reward for evidence-based, reference-quality content over emotional engagement-bait.
- Reduce friction to save: clean typography, sourced data, mobile-legible at a glance.

## 4. Comment engagement (non-spam)

- Genuine prediction polls ("which of these surprises you most?", "which prediction do you disagree with?") outperform static posts and invite real discussion rather than manufactured consensus.
- Questions outperform declarative statements for comment volume. Respond substantively, not with generic "thanks!" replies.

## 5. Tactics that conflict with "receipts, not hype" — explicitly avoid

- Manufactured urgency, countdown gimmicks, "you won't believe this" framing.
- Exaggerated or cherry-picked claims, selective timeframes, missing context. Any gap between claim and evidence shows up in replies and compounds against long-term credibility.
- Acknowledging limitations honestly ("tracked in this region only," "self-reported data") builds more trust than it costs.

## Gaps / unverified

No India-specific research found on social psychology or regional variation in what reads as "credible." Treat the above as a starting default and revisit once the account has its own comment/save data to compare against.
